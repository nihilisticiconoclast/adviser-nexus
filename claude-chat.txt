**The two you couldn't picture**

*The 8 categories → spectral class.* Astronomy already ships a categorical scale for stars: O B A F G K M, blue-white through red. That's seven, and it stretches to eight without strain. The reason it beats an arbitrary 8-colour palette is that it's a **ramp**, so even when you can't name a class you can read which end of it a territory sits at. I've built both, on a toggle, because there's a genuine trade-off and you should let him pick: Spectral reads as ordered but blurs classes D and E in the pale middle; Categorical names all eight cleanly but loses the ordering. My honest recommendation is Categorical for analysis, Spectral for the poster. Either way, 8 hues is at the edge of what anyone discriminates simultaneously — the real answer is clicking a class to isolate, which dims every star *in proportion to how little of its book comes from that channel*. That turns the filter into a map of where each channel actually lives, which is the more useful question anyway.

*Advisers leaving → ejection, not collapse.* A black hole works for a recruiter because the **position persists**: the turf is still there, the gravity is still there, and the book may still orbit. An adviser leaving is the opposite — that mass is gone from the system. So they slip the orbit and drift outward on a fading tail, drained of colour, with the orbital track they used to hold drawn as a broken arc. You can still count what left and see the gap. I tried dimming-in-place first (reads as "poor performer", not "gone") and infall toward the star (implies the firm consumed them, and buries the mark at the centre where it's least visible).

**What the rest encodes**

| Fact | Channel |
|---|---|
| Turf | position, at the centroid |
| Revenue introduced | star radius (√-scaled, normalised to the book) |
| Recruiter tenure | luminosity — corona width and diffraction spikes |
| Adviser tenure | orbital radius, long service pulling in tight |
| Adviser revenue | body radius |
| Joiner channel | hue |
| Recruiter left | black hole, brightest when the loss is recent |

The one addition I made without being asked: **a star's colour is the revenue-weighted blend of its advisers' channels.** A specialist recruiter burns a pure hue; a generalist burns white. It costs nothing to compute and it puts pipeline concentration risk on the map as a visual property rather than a table you have to go and look up. Click Thames Valley in the current data — it's almost pure Channel B, which the inspector calls out as a single point of failure.

**Where this is weak, before he asks**

- **Overplotting in the Midlands and South East.** Orbits pile into each other where turfs are close. Click-to-isolate mitigates it; genuinely fixing it needs either a cartogram (breaks the geography you wanted) or zoom.
- **Three channels compete for the same visual budget.** Size, brightness and hue on one mark is a lot. Brightness is the weakest of the three — if you had to drop one, tenure is better served by a small ring or a numeral than by luminosity.
- **A centroid is a fiction.** Everything sits at the middle of a turf, so the map shows allocation, not where advisers actually are.
- **It's an overview, not an answer.** It's very good at "show me the shape of the estate" and bad at "is Cornwall above or below target" — that's the recruitment charts you already have.
